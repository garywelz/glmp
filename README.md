# 🧬 Genome Logic Modeling Project (GLMP)

**A Comprehensive Analysis of Biological Systems as Computational Programs**

This project demonstrates that **computation is fundamental to all biological systems**. From individual cellular processes to complex developmental programs, from viral decision circuits to circadian clocks, biology implements sophisticated computational logic at every level of organization.

## 🎯 Project Overview

The **Genome Logic Modeling Project (GLMP)** represents a paradigm shift in our understanding of biological systems. By systematically applying the Programming Framework methodology across **371 biological processes**, we have demonstrated that:

- **Biology IS computation** - not just analogous to it
- **Universal computational patterns** exist across all kingdoms of life  
- **Complex behaviors emerge** from well-defined algorithmic processes
- **Engineering principles** can be directly applied to biological systems
- **Predictive models** can be built from computational logic

## 📊 Collection Statistics

- **371 Total Processes** across 36 individual collections
- **6 Kingdoms/Systems** (Viral, Bacterial, Eukaryotic)
- **100% Programming Framework** methodology applied
- **Complete coverage** of major biological computational systems

## 👨‍🔬 Author Information

**Gary Welz**  
*Principal Investigator, Genome Logic Modeling Project*

**Institution:** City University of New York (CUNY)  
- Former Lecturer, Department of Mathematics and Computer Science, John Jay College, CUNY
- Former Lecturer, Borough of Manhattan Community College, CUNY

Gary Welz is a retired mathematics teacher, journalist, web developer and amateur scientist who has spent his life dabbling in various disciplines including exploring the computational nature of biology. His 1995 work on the β-galactosidase regulation system represented one of the first attempts to model genetic regulation using computational logic constructs, creating flowcharts that depicted biological processes as decision trees with conditional branches, feedback loops, and termination conditions.

**Historical Context:** In July 1995, Gary Welz published "Is a Genome Like a Computer Program?" in *The X Advisor*, proposing that computer scientists and biologists should collaborate to describe genomic processes as programming constructs. This early work, discussed on the bionet.genome.chromosome newsgroup with computational biologists including Robert Robbins of Johns Hopkins University, established foundational concepts that continue to influence modern computational biology.

**Current Work:** The Programming Framework methodology represents the culmination of a 30-year evolution in computational biology visualization, from manual 1995 flowcharts to AI-assisted 2025 systematic analysis of 371 biological processes.
