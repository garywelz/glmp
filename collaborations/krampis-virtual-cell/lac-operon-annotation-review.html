What we are asking and why

The Genome Logic Modeling Project (GLMP) proposes that the spatial arrangement of transcription factor binding sites in regulatory DNA encodes logical operations — AND, OR, NOT — analogous to gates in a circuit. We are building a corpus of annotated regulatory sequences in which each binding site is assigned a gate type and circuit complexity class, with coordinates and sequences drawn from primary literature and curated databases.

This approach is complementary to synthetic biology work by Voigt et al. (2016), who demonstrated that logic programs can be compiled into novel DNA sequences that execute gate-level circuits inside living E. coli — writing new regulatory circuits from scratch. GLMP addresses the inverse problem: reading and decoding the logical structure already encoded in natural regulatory DNA by evolution. The two approaches share a grammar of AND, OR, and NOT gates; they differ in direction.

The three operons in this document — lac, ara, and trp — are the first ground-truth entries in that corpus. Before these annotations are used to train or validate the GLMP decoder, we need expert biological review to confirm that the gate assignments and circuit classifications accurately reflect the molecular biology. Your combined expertise in gene regulation, bioinformatics, and microbial biology makes you exactly the right team to do this.

Specific ask — for each of the five annotation entries below:

  1. Sequence — is the binding site sequence correct for E. coli K-12 MG1655?
  2. Coordinates — are the genomic positions in the right range (GenBank U00096.3)?
  3. Gate assignment — does the GLMP logical type (NOT, AND, source node, bistable switch) accurately reflect the molecular biology?
  4. Circuit class — for the lac operon specifically, is Class II or Class III the more accurate designation? (defined in the primer below)
  5. Sources — if you use a different primary reference for any value, please note it so we can reconcile
  6. Omissions — anything about the regulatory logic that the annotation misrepresents or leaves out

Responses welcome in any format — annotated PDF, email, or comments in the GitHub document. If possible, we would appreciate responses by end of August 2026 to inform the next revision of the GLMP methods paper.

GLMP Circuit Complexity Classes — a brief primer

GLMP assigns every regulatory circuit to one of five topology classes based on the presence or absence of feedback cycles in the regulatory subgraph. The class determines how dynamically complex the circuit is and — as shown empirically in the companion paper — how hard it is for AI perturbation models to predict accurately. Class III circuits were systematically harder to predict than Class I circuits across 16 published methods on the K562 Perturb-seq benchmark (t = −3.55, p = 0.0015). Full biological primer →

Positive feedback or mutual repression — all-or-nothing switching

The lac operon classification question in this document concerns whether the CRP-cAMP positive autoregulation loop — when lactose is metabolized, cAMP rises, reinforcing CRP activation and sustaining the ON state — is strong enough to place the lac operon in Class III rather than Class II. The GLMP database currently assigns it Class II; the DNA decoder assigns it Class III. We are asking the review team to weigh in.

NOT gateEntry 1 — lacO1 Operator · LacI repressor

Logical interpretation: LacI binding at lacO1 blocks RNA polymerase access to the lac promoter — the archetypal NOT gate. Repressor binding produces absence of transcription.

Binding site sequence: 5'-AATTGTGAGCGGATAACAATT-3' 21 bp · palindromic · LacI binds as dimer (Gilbert & Maxam 1973; co-crystal structure: Lewis et al. 1996)

Coordinates (U00096.3): 365,906–365,926 · strand − · overlaps lac promoter between −10 element and +1 (RegulonDB v12.0)

Note on circuit-level logic: The ~60 bp center-to-center distance between lacO1 and the CRP site exceeds the GLMP cooperative threshold (~50 bp), so they are OR-independent at the sequence geometry level. However, the promoter requires both inputs simultaneously for strong transcription — AND logic at the circuit level. This two-level interpretation is one of the points we ask reviewers to assess.

Reviewer question: Is the lacO1 sequence and position correct for K-12 MG1655? Is the NOT gate assignment accurately stated? Does the two-level interpretation — OR-independent geometry producing AND circuit logic — accurately reflect the LacI / CRP relationship?
AND inputEntry 2 — CRP Binding Site · catabolite activation

Logical interpretation: The CRP-cAMP complex contacts the RNA polymerase α-subunit C-terminal domain, stimulating open complex formation. Strong lacZYA transcription requires both operator unoccupied (Entry 1) and CRP productively engaged — the AND gate of the lac operon.

Consensus: 5'-TGTGA-N6-TCACA-3' (JASPAR CORE 2024, matrix MA0334)

Lac CRP site (approximate): 5'-TGTGTGGAATTGTGAGC-3' (~22 bp) (RegulonDB v12.0 — reviewer asked to confirm)

Coordinates: Center ~365,966 · −61 relative to +1 (RegulonDB v12.0)

Evidence: Busby & Ebright 1999; Benoff et al. 2002

Reviewer question: Is the CRP site sequence and position correct? Is CRP as AND input — required but not sufficient, acting independently of LacI at the sequence level — accurately stated?
Source nodeEntry 3 — lacI Gene Locus · repressor origin

Logical interpretation: The lacI gene is transcribed constitutively from Plac I producing approximately 10 LacI monomers per cell. LacI assembles as a tetramer capable of bridging lacO1 and an auxiliary operator to form a repression loop. This node is included for perturbation-completeness: CRISPRi targeting lacI, repressor copy number titration, and promoter replacement are experimental levers invisible in a diagram where the repressor simply "exists."

Locus: b0345 · complement(365,163..366,305) · strand − (EcoCyc, U00096.3)

Expression level: ~10 monomers/cell constitutive (Müller-Hill et al. 1968)

Loop formation: LacI tetramer bridges lacO1 and lacO2 or lacO3 (Oehler et al. 1990)

Reviewer question: Is ~10 monomers/cell the current best estimate? Is Plac I largely insensitive to catabolite repression as stated?
Circuit classification question: The GLMP database currently assigns the lac operon Class II (single negative feedback — LacI end-product repression loop). The GLMP DNA decoder assigns it Class III (bistable) based on the CRP positive autoregulation loop: when lac products are metabolized, cAMP levels rise, further activating CRP, reinforcing the ON state. Does this positive reinforcement constitute Class III bistability, or does Class II better describe the dominant regulatory logic? We would value the team's judgment.

Bistable switchEntry 4 — AraC Binding Sites · dual-function NOT→AND

Logical interpretation: AraC performs both repressor and activator functions from a single protein depending on arabinose binding state. In the absence of arabinose, AraC forms a DNA loop between araI1 and araO2, blocking PBAD. When arabinose binds, AraC undergoes a conformational change, releases the loop, and binds araI1-araI2 to activate PBAD. AraC also activates its own transcription (positive autoregulation) — the basis for Class III bistable classification.

Binding sites and approximate positions:
araO2 — single half-site, −270 relative to araBAD TSS (Schleif 1992)
araO1 (O1L, O1R) — two direct-repeat half-sites overlapping araC promoter (Johnson et al. 1988)
araI1, araI2 — direct repeats separated by 4 bp; araI2 overlaps −35 of PBAD (Lee et al. 1987)
CRP site — between araI1 and araO1 (Miyada et al. 1984)

Repression loop: araI1-araO2 (~210 bp) confirmed in vivo (Lobell & Schleif 1990)

Note on FIMO decoder: AraC is absent from JASPAR CORE 2024 and was not recovered by the FIMO scan — expected. Validation requires RegulonDB PWMs for AraC, planned as Phase 1 of the validation work.

Reviewer question: Is the araI1-araO2 loop topology correct? Does positive autoregulation at the araC promoter warrant Class III, or is Class II with an activator switch more accurate? Are the binding site positions consistent with your preferred reference for K-12 MG1655?

NOT gateEntry 5 — TrpR Binding Site + Translational Attenuation

Logical interpretation: TrpR alone (aporepressor) cannot bind the trp operator — only the TrpR-tryptophan holorepressor binds trpO and blocks RNAP (~300-fold repression). A second independent mechanism, translational attenuation, provides approximately 8-fold additional regulation: when tryptophan-charged tRNA is abundant, ribosomes translate the 162-nucleotide trpL leader rapidly, a terminator hairpin forms in the nascent RNA, and transcription halts before the structural genes. When tryptophan is scarce, ribosomes stall at two consecutive Trp codons (UGG-UGG), an anti-terminator hairpin forms instead, and RNA polymerase reads through.

Operator consensus: 5'-CTAG(N4)CTAG-3' (Otwinowski et al. 1988)

trpO (approximate — please verify): 5'-TGTACTAGTTAACTAGTACG-3' approximately −23 to −3 relative to trpEDCBA TSS (reviewer asked to confirm against RegulonDB v12.0 or EcoCyc)

Locus: ~1,316–1,323 kb in U00096.3 (trpE region)

TrpR repression: ~300-fold (EcoCyc trpLEDCBA, accessed June 2026)

Attenuation: ~8-fold additional; alternate RNA hairpin structures depend on ribosome position at UGG-UGG codons in trpL (Yanofsky 1981; Oxender et al. 1979; Landick & Yanofsky 1987)

trpR autoregulation: TrpR-Trp also represses its own gene — trpR operator and promoter overlap (Gunsalus & Yanofsky 1980)

Reviewer question: Is the trpO sequence and position correct for K-12 MG1655? Is 300-fold the right figure for TrpR repression? Is attenuation correctly characterized as a second independent regulatory layer rather than part of the same NOT gate? Should attenuation be represented as a distinct element in the GLMP circuit class schema?
